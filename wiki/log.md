# Run log

## 2026-04-12 — session 1

### Phase 1: pure classical baseline

- **exp001** confirms bare charged shell collapses; Earnshaw's theorem in action.
- **exp002** shows rigid rotation stabilizes at any positive L, with correct Z^2 scaling, but L=1 (one natural unit) gives hydrogen off by factor 2/3. Fit L = sqrt(2/3) gives hydrogen and all hydrogenic ions exactly.
- **exp003** surveys alternative classical geometries (ring, point orbit, solid ball, double shell). The RING and POINT-ON-ORBIT models give hydrogen exactly with L=1. The SHELL is the awkward one. Cleanest rescue: replace rigid rotation with an empirical 1/R² barrier of strength α=1/2.
- **exp004** confirms that surface tension, 1/R^n polytropic pressures (n≠2), etc. all fail the Z^2 scaling test. Only 1/R^2 barriers survive.

### Phase 2: hydrogenic series

- **exp005** confirms the chosen one-electron theory (T=1/(2R²) barrier, V=-Z/R) reproduces Z=1..10 exactly.

### Phase 3: helium, many branches

- **exp006** surveys 7 geometric configurations. Best: antipodal points (5.5%).
- **exp007** adds 3 fit-based theories and more geometric variants. Best parameter-free: 120-deg arrangement (0.88%). Best with 1 fit: variational Z_eff (exact by construction).

### Phase 4: lithium

- **exp008** tests 4 structural hypotheses × 4 same-shell λ values.
  - L1 (all three in one shell) gives LOWER energy than L2 in pure classical physics, but disagrees with experiment by 16-25%. **Without an occupancy rule, classical physics predicts lithium overbinds**.
  - L2 (2+1) with the He-fit λ lands within 4.45% — but L2 is NOT the classical minimum without further rules.
  - L3 (three independent shells) gives 6.4% with no correlation. Surprising but real.
  - L4 (1+2 flipped) gives 0.67% with the 120° rule — suspiciously good, possibly accidental.

### Phase 5: generalized shell model, Be..Ne

- **exp009** tries the postulate pair:
  1. Each electron carries a "principal" label n. Shell n has kinetic barrier T_n(R) = n² / (2R²) per electron. (Equivalent to "L_n = n" in the rigid-ring picture.)
  2. Shell n holds at most 2n² electrons.
  3. Electrons inside a shell arrange on the Thomson polyhedron (classical electrostatic minimum).
- **Result**: ALL 10 atoms within 5.5%; the worst is He (5.49%, antipodal overbinds) and the best is C (0.12%).
- NO fitting parameters. This is a classical toy model of the periodic table's first row.
